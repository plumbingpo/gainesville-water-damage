What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Gainesville, FL

Home / Blog / What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Gainesville, FL

For most homeowners, a pipe bursting ranks among the worst plumbing emergencies there are. Water pours out of a burst line at a rate of 4 to 8 gallons a minute, so within hours rooms may be flooded, possessions damaged, and building materials weakened. Knowing exactly what to do in the first critical minutes can save you thousands of dollars in damage and separate a manageable repair from a major restoration project. Gainesville Restoration put together these steps so Gainesville, FL residents know exactly how to respond to this common problem.

Step 1: Turn the Water Off Right Away

Your first job after a pipe breaks is simple: get the water shut off as soon as you possibly can. The longer it runs, the more you lose and the more you pay. Start by finding the main shutoff valve for the house. Homes in Gainesville usually have it close to the meter: check the basement, the garage, a utility room, or an outside wall facing the street. To stop water throughout the house, twist the handle clockwise (righty-tighty) until it stops. If you can identify the specific pipe that burst and it has its own shutoff valve nearby, close that valve as well.

Your water company can cut the supply at the meter, so call them at once if the valve is missing or will not budge. This is one of the most important reasons to know your shutoff valve location BEFORE an emergency. Go look for it today, then check that the handle turns without a fight. A valve that will not move should be repaired by a plumber now, not during a flood.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

Before the crew arrives, you can cut your losses, starting with getting documents, electronics, and valuables out of the wet zone. Lift furniture off wet carpet by placing aluminum foil or plastic blocks under legs. Take area rugs off floors that got wet. Where puddles are easy to get to, blot them up with towels. Prop cabinet and closet doors open so air can move through. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker box, but only when that is safe and you are not standing in water.

Do not step into pooled water anywhere close to outlets, electrical panels, or appliances. When flooding is heavy and there is no safe route to the panel, do not try to shut the power off; let professionals evaluate it first. Your safety matters more than the house.

Step 4: Record the Damage for Your Claim

Your insurance claim will need evidence, so record everything carefully before any cleaning starts. Capture the affected belongings, damaged materials, and standing water in both photos and video. Note the date and approximate time you discovered the burst pipe, and document the source of the leak if visible. This documentation, combined with the detailed reports Gainesville Restoration provides, will support your insurance claim and help you seek fair compensation for the damage.

Preventing Burst Pipes in Gainesville

While not all pipe bursts can be prevented, Gainesville homeowners can significantly reduce their risk by insulating exposed pipes in unheated areas like garages, attics, and crawl spaces. During cold weather, keep faucets dripping slightly to maintain water movement. Warm room air should be able to reach pipes on exterior walls, so open the cabinets under those sinks. Monitor your water pressure and install a pressure reducing valve if pressure exceeds 80 PSI. Replace aging supply lines, especially rubber washing machine hoses, with braided stainless steel. Above all, make sure you can find your main shutoff long before an emergency.
