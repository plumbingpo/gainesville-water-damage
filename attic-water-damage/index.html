Attic Water Damage Help in Gainesville

Attic water damage comes from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Gainesville inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • Ceilings upstairs with brownish stains or rings that grow following storms
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Insulation that is soaked or has mold gets bagged and carried out, giving the wood and the drywall below a chance to dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Wood

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Fix the Cause

The roof or ventilation issue behind it is pointed out to you, and we are glad to pass what we found to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

Air Conditioner Drips

Air handlers, with their drain pans and condensate lines, sit in many attics. When the pan overflows or the drain clogs, insulation gets wet for weeks without any rain as a warning.

Water Near Electrical

Cable runs, junction boxes and recessed light housings are all found in attics. Do not enter the attic before someone checks the power, because wet electrical parts can shock you or start a fire.

Mold on Sheathing

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.
